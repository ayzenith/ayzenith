Part I

Who We Sell To

We do not sell to everyone, and we do not try to. The quality of our book depends first on the quality of the partners we choose to pursue.

The best partnerships begin with a conversation, not a transaction.
AYZENITH Sales PlaybookPart I — Who We Sell To
01 · Ideal Customer Profile

The partners we are built to serve

Our ideal customer is a serious business that wants to source or move products across borders, and values a reliable partner more than the lowest possible price.

AYZENITH creates the most value for buyers who feel the exact problem we exist to solve: real opportunity abroad, but unacceptable exposure to distance, opacity and risk. The strongest fits share a recognisable profile.

Retail & wholesale buyers

Chains, distributors and wholesalers who need dependable stock, consistent quality and no supply surprises across a full cycle.

E-commerce & marketplace sellers

Operators scaling volume who need reliable sourcing and fulfilment, and want to move up the value chain toward private label.

Corporate & procurement buyers

Organisations that require documentation, compliance and a single accountable partner rather than a chain of brokers.

Brand builders

Businesses ready to develop own-brand products on proven demand — owning the margin instead of renting it.

What makes a strong fit

  • Values reliability and accountability over the cheapest quote.
  • Operates at a volume and category we can genuinely serve well.
  • Thinks in relationships and repeat business, not one-off deals.
  • Operates legitimately and expects to work within proper frameworks.
Focus before breadth

We concentrate on categories we understand well enough to guarantee. A buyer in a category we cannot yet serve properly is not a poor prospect — they are simply an early one. We say so honestly.

02 · Customer Qualification

Qualifying honestly, early

Qualification protects both sides. We would rather decline a poor fit early than disappoint a partner we should never have taken on. Our name is on every outcome.

We qualify against five dimensions. The goal is not to filter people out; it is to understand quickly whether AYZENITH is the right partner for what they are trying to do.

DimensionWhat we are listening for
NeedA real sourcing, distribution, private-label or partnership need — not idle price-checking.
Category fitA category we can serve to our standard today, or a credible path to one.
ScaleVolume and ambition that justify a genuine partnership, now or over time.
ValuesA buyer who weighs reliability and accountability, not price alone.
LegitimacyA business that operates properly and expects to work within legal and regulatory frameworks.

Qualify in when

  • The buyer describes a recurring need we can own end to end.
  • They care how problems are handled, not only what things cost.
  • The category matches our sourcing depth.

Decline or defer when

  • The only question is who is cheapest.
  • The category is one we cannot yet guarantee.
  • The request sits outside proper legal or regulatory frameworks.
The standard

A declined inquiry is still a brand impression. We decline with respect and clarity, and where we can, we point the buyer somewhere useful. Reputation compounds in both directions.

03 · The Sales Funnel

How a partnership takes shape

Our funnel is deliberately narrow and deep. We would rather carry fewer, better opportunities through with care than push volume we cannot serve.

1

Identify

Find businesses that match the Ideal Customer Profile — through inbound inquiries, referrals, and considered outbound outreach.

2

Engage

Open a relevant, low-pressure conversation. The aim is a first conversation, never an immediate sale.

3

Qualify

Confirm fit against the five dimensions. Move forward together, or part on good terms.

4

Discover

Understand the need in depth — category, scale, constraints and the real objective behind the request.

5

Propose & align

Present a clear proposal; work through questions honestly toward a durable agreement.

6

Commit

Close cleanly and hand to operations with full context, so the first order proves the promise.

7

Grow

Turn a first order into a lasting account (Part IV). The funnel does not end at the close; it deepens.

04 · Prospecting Strategy

Finding the right partners

We prospect for fit, not for volume. A smaller number of well-chosen, well-researched approaches will always outperform mass outreach — and it protects the brand.

Where good prospects come from

  • Referrals — the highest-quality source. A satisfied partner's introduction carries our reliability with it.
  • Inbound inquiries — buyers who found us and already understand the positioning. We reply within two business days.
  • Considered outbound — specific businesses in our categories that would genuinely benefit from what we do.
  • Trade networks & fairs — sector events where serious buyers and suppliers gather (see Brand Book, Trade Fair Identity).

How we prepare before reaching out

Every approach begins with research. We learn the prospect's category, scale, likely sourcing pain and a specific, credible reason AYZENITH is relevant to them. If we cannot articulate that reason in one honest sentence, we are not ready to reach out.

The prospecting test

Before any outreach: would this message be useful to the recipient even if they never reply? If not, we refine it until it is. We earn attention; we do not demand it.

05 · LinkedIn Outreach

Professional, patient, relevant

LinkedIn is a place to build professional standing, not to pitch. We show up as a credible institution and open conversations we would be glad to have in person.

How we operate

  • Connect with a short, specific, genuine note — never a copy-paste pitch.
  • Lead with relevance to their business, not a description of ours.
  • Give the relationship room; a first message aims for a conversation, not a contract.
  • Support our words with a professional presence (see Brand Book, Social Media Identity).
Connection note — example

Hello [First name] — I lead partnerships at AYZENITH, a Türkiye-based sourcing and trade group. I follow work in [category] and thought it would be worth connecting. No agenda; happy to be a useful contact if cross-border sourcing ever becomes relevant for [Company].

First message after connecting — example

Thanks for connecting, [First name]. I won't pitch you — I only wanted to open the door. We help businesses in [category] source and move products across borders with one accountable partner for quality, logistics and delivery. If that's ever useful, I'm glad to share how we work. If not, it's good to be connected regardless.

Tone

Calm and confident. We never chase, never flatter, never manufacture urgency. A partner worth keeping is worth waiting for.

06 · Email Outreach

Clear, specific, easy to say yes to

A good outreach email respects the reader's time: it is short, it is relevant, and it asks for one small, reasonable next step.

Structure of a strong email

  1. Relevant opening — a specific, true reason we are writing to them.
  2. What we do, in one line — the value, not the mechanics.
  3. Why it may matter to them — tied to their category or situation.
  4. One clear, low-commitment ask — usually a short conversation.
Cold email — example

Subject: Sourcing [category] across borders — one accountable partner

Hello [First name],

I lead partnerships at AYZENITH, a Türkiye-based B2B sourcing and trade group. We help businesses like [Company] source and move [category] internationally — handling vetting, quality control, customs and delivery as one accountable relationship, so you gain access without carrying the risk.

If reliable cross-border supply is something you're working on this year, I'd welcome a short conversation to understand your needs and tell you honestly whether we're the right fit. Would a 20-minute call in the next week or two be useful?

Either way, thank you for your time.
— [Name], AYZENITH · info@ayzenith.com

Do

  • Keep it under 150 words.
  • Make the first line specific to them.
  • Offer an easy, respectful next step.

Don't

  • Use false urgency or "limited spots".
  • Send generic blasts at scale.
  • Over-claim results or scale.

07 · Discovery Meetings

Understand before you propose

The discovery meeting is the most important conversation in the sale. Our job is to understand the partner's world well enough to tell them honestly how — or whether — we can help.

We listen more than we speak. A good discovery leaves the prospect feeling understood, not sold to. We aim to leave every discovery meeting able to answer four questions.

What are they really trying to do?

The business objective behind the request — growth, reliability, margin, a new market.

What is the pressure?

The pain that makes this urgent — supplier failure, quality issues, complexity, risk.

What does success look like?

How they will measure whether a partner solved the problem.

Can we serve this properly?

Whether AYZENITH can genuinely own this outcome — and the honest answer if not.

Questions we ask

  • What are you sourcing or moving today, and where does it get difficult?
  • What has gone wrong with previous suppliers or partners?
  • What volumes and categories matter most over the next year?
  • If this worked exactly as you hoped, what would change for your business?

08 · Proposal Structure

A proposal that states what we own

A strong AYZENITH proposal removes ambiguity. It tells the partner exactly what we will do, to what standard, and where our responsibility begins and ends.

1

Understanding

A short restatement of the partner's objective and constraints — proof we listened.

2

Scope

The specific capabilities we will provide: sourcing, distribution, private label, or partnership.

3

Standards

The disciplines behind the work — vetting, pre-shipment inspection, compliance, tracked logistics.

4

Responsibilities

What AYZENITH owns and what the partner owns. No silent assumptions.

5

Terms

Transparent commercial terms, structured for a relationship that holds across a full cycle.

6

Next steps

A clear, low-friction path to a first order.

Governing rule

We never promise in a proposal what operations cannot deliver. A proposal is the first physical form of our promise — it must be exactly true.

09 · Negotiation Principles

Negotiate toward a durable agreement

We negotiate to build a relationship that both sides want to keep — not to extract the maximum from a single deal. A cornered partner is not a lasting one.

01

Anchor on value, not price

We compete on reliability. We explain what accountability and de-risking are worth before we discuss cost.

02

Be transparent

Clear terms build trust faster than clever ones. We do not win through confusion.

03

Protect the relationship

We would rather hold a fair position than win an unfair one that damages the partnership later.

04

Know our floor

We understand the terms below which the work cannot be done to our standard — and we hold them calmly.

05

Walk away well

If terms cannot support the standard our name requires, we decline with respect. A good "no" protects the brand.

10 · Objection Handling

Meeting concerns with candour

An objection is a request for reassurance, not a rejection. We answer honestly — and if a concern is legitimate, we acknowledge it rather than argue.

What we hearHow we respond
"Another supplier is cheaper."Acknowledge it, then explain what the price does not include: vetting, quality control, accountability when something goes wrong. We compete on reliability, and we let the buyer weigh it.
"We already have suppliers."Good — we are not asking them to replace what works. We ask where it gets difficult, and offer to be the accountable partner for that part, or a dependable second source.
"How do we know we can trust you?"A fair question. We describe exactly how we operate, start with a controlled first order, and let performance earn the relationship.
"You're a young company."We are early, and we treat it as an advantage: partners who build with us now build at the founding, with direct access and full attention.
"We're not ready yet."Then there is no pressure. We stay in touch as a useful contact and are ready when the need is real.
Rule

We never handle an objection by overstating a capability. If we cannot answer a concern truthfully in our favour, the honest answer is the right one.

11 · Follow-up Process

Persistence without pressure

Most partnerships are not won in the first conversation. We follow up consistently and respectfully — adding value each time, never simply "checking in".

1

Same-day summary

After every meeting, a short note restating what we heard and the agreed next step.

2

Value-added follow-ups

Each subsequent contact brings something useful — an answer, an option, a relevant insight — not just a reminder.

3

Respectful cadence

Spaced, professional, never daily. We make it easy to re-engage and easy to say "not now".

4

The graceful pause

If the timing is wrong, we say we will stay in touch — and we do, without pestering.

Follow-up after a quiet period — example

Hello [First name] — no pressure at all, just keeping the door open. If [category] sourcing has moved up your priorities, I'm glad to pick up where we left off. If the timing still isn't right, that's completely fine — I'll remain a useful contact whenever it is.

12 · Closing Strategy

Closing as a natural conclusion

When we have qualified well, understood deeply and proposed honestly, closing is not a manoeuvre — it is the natural next step. We make it easy, clear and pressure-free.

How we close

  • Confirm alignment — restate what we have agreed and check nothing is unresolved.
  • Remove friction — make the first step small, concrete and easy to take.
  • Propose a first order — a controlled, well-scoped first engagement that lets performance earn the future.
  • Set expectations — exactly what happens next, and by when, once they say yes.
What we never do

No artificial deadlines, no discount pressure, no "sign today" tactics. If a partner needs time, time is part of the relationship. Trust closed under pressure does not last.

A first order is a promise we get to keep. That is how the real selling begins.

After the Yes

The sale is the beginning, not the end. Our most important selling happens after the first order — in how we deliver, how we handle problems, and how we grow the relationship over years.

AYZENITH Sales PlaybookPart IV — After the Yes
13 · Relationship Building

Turning a buyer into a partner

Every promise kept compounds. The first flawless delivery does more for the relationship than any pitch — and it is where trust becomes real.

Relationship building is deliberate work, not a by-product. We stay close to partners between orders, understand how their business is changing, and look for ways to be more useful before we are asked. We are present when things go wrong, and we communicate the way this company communicates: precise, calm and honest.

Be reliable, visibly

On time, in full, defect-free — consistently. Reliability is the relationship.

Stay in touch with purpose

Regular, useful contact between orders — not only when we want something.

Own problems fully

When something goes wrong, we are the partner still standing there. That is when loyalty is earned.

Look ahead for them

Anticipate needs, surface options, and help partners grow into new categories.

14 · Long-Term Account Management

Growing accounts the right way

A well-served account grows on its own momentum — from a first order into more categories, larger volumes, private label and exclusive supply. Our job is to earn each step.

Account stageOur focusWhat unlocks growth
New accountDeliver the first orders flawlessly; set the standard.Consistency and honest communication.
Established accountDeepen categories and volume; forecast together.Reliability under pressure; proactive service.
Strategic accountPrivate label, exclusive supply, joint planning.Proven trust and aligned long-term goals.
Partner-of-recordCo-development; AYZENITH as the default partner.Years of kept promises.
Discipline

We never grow an account faster than we can serve it. Expanding a relationship into a category we cannot yet guarantee risks the trust the whole account is built on. Responsible growth applies to accounts, not just to the company.

15 · Sales KPIs

What we measure — and why

We measure the health of relationships, not just the volume of transactions. Our metrics are chosen to reward long-term partnership over short-term pressure.

Fit
Share of new partners matching the Ideal Customer Profile
Repeat
Proportion of revenue from returning partners
Retention
Partners retained year over year
Growth
Expansion within existing accounts

The metrics we watch

KPIWhat it tells us
Qualified inquiry response timeWhether we honour our two-business-day standard.
Discovery-to-proposal rateHow well we qualify and understand before proposing.
First-order success rateWhether operations delivered on what sales promised.
Repeat & expansion revenueThe truest measure — partners choosing us again.
Partner retentionWhether relationships last, which is the whole strategy.
Referral rateWhether partners trust us enough to introduce us.
What we refuse to optimise for

We do not chase vanity volume, one-off wins, or metrics that reward pressure over patience. A number that improves at the cost of a relationship is a number moving the wrong way.

We compete on reliability, not price — and reliability is the only metric that compounds.
